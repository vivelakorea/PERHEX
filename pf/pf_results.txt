Junction-graph extraction + midpoint hex mesh v0 -- results summary
====================================================================
Scripts: pf_topology.py (extraction + Euler gate), pf_hexmesh.py (v0 mesh).
Outputs: pf_snap_f*_complex.npz (verified complex), pf_snap_f*_mesh.npz
(coords [unit cube, wrapped], conn [0-based hex8], grain, minsj, mindet).
Runtime: topology ~3 s, hexmesh <1 s per frame (all vectorized, 80^3 grid).

Method (and why gid-seeded, not OP-threshold-seeded)
----------------------------------------------------
The partition is rebuilt from the SMOOTH order parameters: voxel grain =
argmax of cell-averaged OPs on a 2x supersampled (80^3) grid (trilinear
nodal interpolation), with GrainTracker's raw per-element gid used ONLY to
disambiguate OP reuse (majority vote per periodic dominant-OP component).
Raw gid alone is unusable (~4000 speckle islands per frame inside diffuse
interfaces); OP top-k thresholding alone was the old pf_junctions_op.py
approach (5/24 Euler) whose threshold provably merges clusters along short
triple lines or drops weak quad points. argmax needs NO threshold, and the
supersampling resolves most sub-voxel features. All topology below is exact
combinatorics of that clean periodic partition; instance multiplicity comes
from periodic connected components, never from grain-set identity.

Entities (all periodic):
- face instance: component of interface voxel-faces of one pair, joined
  across 2-grain edges; 3-grain "pinch" edges (cyclic pattern a,b,a,c = two
  grains touching only diagonally) are resolved non-manifold ridges: they
  are NOT triple lines, and the two same-pair face wedges are joined
  through them (resolved-manifold interpretation).
- tri instance: component of TRUE triple edges (3-grain, 3 transitions
  around the edge cycle), joined across 3-grain corners; ends at junction
  corners.
- vertex: connected junction blob of >=4-grain corners (joined through
  U>=4 edges / pinch edges / nested-set staircase adjacency; a true triple
  edge between non-nested corner sets is a legitimate length-1 bridge and
  keeps vertices split).

Degeneracy repairs (all detected and applied automatically, iteratively):
1. chord removal: <=2-edge arcs starting AND ending in one blob are chords
   of the contracted vertex -> deleted.
2. vertex split: a >2-edge arc with both ends at two DIFFERENT corners of
   one blob means the blob wrongly merged two vertices -> forbid those
   direct joins, rebuild.
3. bundle merge: >=2 parallel <=2-edge arcs between the same two blobs
   cannot be generic -> the blobs are one (degenerate) junction -> merged.
4. rim-less micro-face absorption: a face instance with no bordering arcs
   whose pair is contained in the single junction blob surrounding it is
   sub-voxel junction detail -> absorbed into the blob.
Vertices with >4 grains are flagged degenerate (honest: real >4 junctions
or mid-topological-transition states, e.g. f3 has one 5-grain vertex
{2,6,11,13,20} where 5 OPs are all 0.18-0.36 -- a T1-type event in flight).

Where the curved construction fails (the v0 finding, later resolved):
ALL inverted hexes sat at quad points with r_qp/r_grain = 1.0-1.8, at
corners BEYOND the grain's equivalent-sphere radius, with minSJ ~ -0.9.

v1 RESOLUTION: those hexes were not folded at all -- they were LEFT-HANDED
cells misclassified by the v0 handedness test. v0 decided flips from the
q-corner tetra det(m1-q, m2-q, m3-q), which at thin far corners is a
near-degenerate sliver whose sign (~+1) disagrees with the cell volume
(~-100s): corner diagnosis showed corner 0 det ~ 0..+2 and ALL seven other
corners strongly negative. Deciding handedness by CELL VOLUME (sum of
Gauss dets) fixes every case:

CPFEM SMOKE TEST (part 3)
-------------------------
Deck: pf_deck.py -> pf_cpfem/pf_cpfem.inp from pf_snap_f3_mesh_n2p.npz
(f3, n=2 graded, OP-PROJECTED, 4608 C3D8R, minSJ 0.220). Wrap-style PBC
per neper2abaqus.py: 902 image nodes (elements crossing the cube), 2706
*Equations u_img - u_prim = shift . jumps, 3 dummy nodes (dummy-3 =
normal jumps). Materials: OXFORD-UMAT 300-constant Al (oxford_props
write_material), random Bunge angles seed 42, 24 grains. UMAT sources
copied from oxford_mp/ (dbconvect already 0; db_table.dat / gb_rt.bin NOT
copied -- both reads are inquire-guarded, so the GB storage term is
inactive = upstream-OXFORD behavior). Load: uniaxial x tension to 2% at
1e-3/s, Static, nlgeom.

Result: COMPLETED SUCCESSFULLY. 103 increments, exactly 1 cutback (the
very first increment, 0.1 -> 0.05 s; everything after converged in 1-3
equilibrium iterations, no further cutbacks). Wall clock 28 s on 8 cpus.
No element-quality-related solver behavior at all: no warnings tied to
the minSJ~0.22 graded slivers, no negative-eigenvalue or hourglass
noise in the .msg at 2% strain.

PBC jump check (pf_post.py, final frame): max residual over all 902
image-pair equations = 1.9e-9 (machine zero). Macro jumps: X =
(0.02, 0, 0) exactly as applied; lateral Y2 = -0.0103, Z3 = -0.0091
(transverse sum ~ -0.0194 vs +0.02 axial: near-incompressible plastic
contraction, uniaxial-stress state confirmed).

Volume-weighted avg S11 (reference volumes; graded mesh spreads element
volume ~1e3x so weighting matters): yield ~8 MPa then hardening to
14.3 MPa at 2% (sscurve_pf.csv). Sanity: consistent with the Rubio/
Haouala annealed-Al parameter set WITHOUT the GB storage term (tau_c0 =
gf*mu*b*sqrt(12*rho0) ~ 2.7 MPa -> sigma_y ~ 8 MPa). The parent
oxford_mp run shows 59.7 MPa at 2% -- expected difference: that run has
the GB term active (db_table present, ks=5) and a different texture/
microstructure; ours is the deliberately GB-inactive baseline.

T4: 8-GRAIN DOMAIN-SCALE-CURVATURE RUN (gg_fewgrains_out.e, op_num=8)
=====================================================================
(extract_pf.py generalized: python extract_pf.py <frame> <exodus>)
11 frames, 8 grains -> 4 (f7) -> 1-2 (f9+). Processed f0-f7 topologies;
mesh/CPFEM targets f5 (t=11428, 8 grains, strongest curvature) and f7
(t=14788, 4 grains).

Topology gate -- GENERALIZED by this stress test, as designed. T4 exposed
two false assumptions in the T1-era gate and one real bug:
1. Faces can be ANNULI (rim = 2 closed loops; chi_face = 2 - n_loops).
   e.g. f5 face {1,3}: a belt around a grain. The instance extraction was
   already correct; only the gate arithmetic assumed disks.
2. Grains can PERCOLATE (wrap the torus). Wrap rank w (rank of the
   self-adjacency offset lattice, computed per grain) does NOT determine
   boundary genus: f5 grain 3 (71% matrix, w=3) has chi(dg)=-4 (genus 3),
   but f7 grain 3 (94%, w=3) is "torus minus a ball cluster" with
   SPHERE boundary chi=2. So the gate now compares against the
   INDEPENDENTLY computed voxel-surface Euler characteristic chi_vox(dg)
   (F - sum_edge g-runs + sum_corner wedge-sheets, non-manifold resolved):
      V - E + F - n_annuli == chi_vox(dg), no odd-degree face rims.
   No topology assumptions left in the gate.
3. Writing chi_vox exposed a numpy trap (bool+bool = OR, undercounting
   2-run pinch edges); validated against synthetic shapes (voxel, cube,
   L-tromino, flat ring chi=0, edge-touch pair chi=4, corner-touch pair).
Result: ALL T4 frames pass 8/8 (f7: 4/4), including percolating grains
and annular faces. T1 frames re-verified against chi_vox: all pass.

Meshing -- the honest T4 finding:
- EVERY T4 frame (f0 included) contains >=1 percolating grain (f0/f1:
  grains 3,7 w=1; f2+: grain 3 w=2..3, growing to 94% volume). A
  percolating grain has non-ball topology, so the single-apex midpoint
  construction is TOPOLOGICALLY IMPOSSIBLE for it (a hex fan from one
  center only closes over a sphere boundary). pf_hexmesh now detects
  grain_wrap and skips such grains explicitly.
- f5 partial mesh (7 ball grains, 28.6% of volume): 80 coarse hexes,
  4 inverted after volume flip -- the first TRUE geometric folds seen
  (T1's were all handedness artifacts): they sit on the two grains with
  ANNULAR faces, whose global centroid is a bad geometry sample (an
  annulus centroid can sit over its hole). Apex move insufficient;
  bounded untangler (<=2 vox) fixed 1 of 4, leaving 3 inverted
  (minSJ -0.12). n=2 refinement inherits and projection WORSENS them
  (proj displacement mean 0.80 / max 4.1 vox on strongly curved faces
  vs 0.16-0.24 on T1/T3 -- the flat-centroid sample is far from the
  iso-surface at domain-scale curvature).
- f7: 6 hexes (94% unmeshed), valid but poor (median SJ 0.17).
- CPFEM for T4: BLOCKED, precisely: no frame yields a full conforming
  periodic mesh (percolating matrix at every frame; meshable subset
  <= 28.6% of volume is not a closed RVE). v2 path: decompose non-ball
  grains (multi-apex / medial split) and per-sector face samples
  instead of one global face centroid.

Qualitative difference vs T1-f3 baseline: extraction+gate handle extreme
curvature fully (annuli, genus, percolation all detected and verified);
the MESH CONSTRUCTION is what breaks -- first at annular-face geometry
samples (folds), then structurally at percolation (unmeshable). T1-like
polycrystals (all ball grains, disk faces) mesh with 0 inverted.

Conclusions:
- The topology extraction + generalized Euler gate (V-E+F-annuli ==
  chi_vox) is unconditionally robust across all four microstructure
  classes, including percolation, annular faces, and dying grains.
- The midpoint hex construction is robust for ball-grain polycrystals of
  any size disparity tested (up to 5020x element volumes, 2067x grain
  volumes) and fails only where cell topology stops being ball-like
  (T4): that is a topology-adaptation problem (multi-apex decomposition,
  per-sector face samples), not a numerical one.
- Abaqus/Standard + OXFORD-UMAT is indifferent to the graded midpoint
  meshes' quality range (minSJ 0.08-0.27) and volume spreads: every
  runnable case converged with the same 103-increment / 1-cutback
  signature at 12-28 s.
